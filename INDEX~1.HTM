What am I looking at?
The three meters — what kind of market this is
Trending — price is moving directionally. Breakouts and pullbacks work.
Ranging — chopping sideways. Fades and reversals work.
Volatile — big erratic swings, no clean direction. Stops get hit on noise.
%The three always add up to 100%. Each meter has its own 0–100% scale and the strongest is highlighted and marked “strongest”, so you never have to compare bar widths yourself.
How to act on it
55+A meter above 55% is a clear regime you can lean on. Match your setup to it: trending → breakouts and pullbacks, ranging → fades and reversals.
45–55Only a lean. The better bet, but a thinner edge — take fewer trades.
<45If nothing reaches 45%, no regime is in charge. Switching styles costs the most here; waiting is legitimate.
⚠Volatile overrides the other two. Above 35% cut your size whatever else the meters say; when it is the strongest meter, the honest answer is usually no trade.
ⓘThe app writes the interpretation in plain English directly underneath the meters and updates it every scan. The rules above are so you can check its reasoning rather than take it on faith.
The two numbers — and why they differ
ⓘThe big number is raw market health, built from the six ingredients listed under the meters. The number next to the verdict is that same score after adjusting for things health cannot see: trading session, dollar strength, your daily loss limit, and whether the scanner found anything. The line at the top of this box shows the arithmetic, so the two can never look like they disagree.
🟢GO — 15+ above your floor. Healthy: liquidity, trend clarity and calm funding line up. Normal sizing is defensible.
🟡CAUTION — at or just above your floor. Workable but mixed: be choosier and trade smaller.
🔴STAND DOWN — below your floor, or your daily loss limit is hit. Most edges evaporate here; waiting is a position.

Risk terms explained
HeatPortfolio heat — total % of your account you would lose if every open stop hit at once. This is the number that ends accounts.
GrossCombined size of all positions. Large gross with low heat still means large exposure to a gap.
NetLongs minus shorts. Near zero means you are roughly market-neutral.
ClusterPositions that tend to move together (most alts follow BTC). Five correlated trades is closer to one big trade than five separate ones.
RR = one unit of risk. +2R means you made twice what you risked; −1R is a normal full stop-out.

How to read your edge stats
RR multiple — result in units of risk, not dollars. This lets a $20 trade and a $2,000 trade be compared fairly.
ExpectancyAverage R per trade. Positive means your process makes money over time, even with losing streaks. This is the single most important number here.
PFProfit factor — gross wins ÷ gross losses. Above 1.3 is healthy; below 1.0 loses money.
Win %Deliberately not the headline. A 40% win rate at +2R beats a 70% win rate at +0.3R.
CurveCumulative profit in order. Look for steady slope, not the final number.
📊R distribution — how results cluster. A long left tail means losses are running past their stops.
FeelingWin rate grouped by the emotion you logged. If "fomo" trades lose, that is the most actionable stat in the app.

What do the speed tabs (Scalp → Swing) and the profiles actually change?
SCALP / DAY / SWING — how fast you trade
These tabs change which candles the scanner reads. Every rule stays identical — but stops, targets and trade duration scale with the candle size.

⚡ Scalp — tap it and:
· Signals are built from 5-minute candles, trend confirmed on 15-minute
· It studies roughly the last 21 hours of price action
· Stops & targets become tight (fractions of a percent) — trades resolve in minutes to a few hours
· Most signals, most noise. Only for when you're actively watching.

☀️ Day — tap it and:
· Signals from 15-minute candles, trend confirmed on 1-hour
· It studies roughly the last 2–3 days
· Stops & targets are medium — trades resolve in hours, usually inside a day
· Best balance of quality vs frequency. The default.

🌤 30m — tap it and:
· Signals from 30-minute candles, trend confirmed on 4-hour
· Studies roughly the last 5 days of price action
· Trades typically resolve in hours to a couple of days — check in a few times a day.

⛅ 1h — tap it and:
· Signals from 1-hour candles, trend confirmed on 4-hour
· Studies roughly the last 10 days of price action
· Trades typically resolve in 1–4 days — a relaxed pace without full swing patience.

📅 Swing — tap it and:
· Signals from 4-hour candles, trend confirmed on the daily
· It studies roughly the last 6 weeks
· Stops & targets get wide (often several percent) — trades resolve in days to weeks
· Fewest signals, slowest moving. Right if you only check once or twice a day.

CONSV / BALANCED / AGGR — how picky it is
These tabs change the standards a setup must pass. The indicators are the same — the bar to qualify moves.

Conservative — tap it and:
· Only trend-following setups allowed (Pullbacks & Breakouts). Bounce/rejection setups are switched off
· Price must be on the correct side of the 200-bar average (no longs in a downtrend)
· Minimum reward-to-risk 1 : 2 — a setup risking $5 must target ≥$10
· STRONG badge needs score ≥ 70 · tightest RSI band (45–68)
· Result: the fewest signals, the highest quality.

Balanced — tap it and:
· Adds Oversold Bounce & Overbought Rejection setups
· 200-bar average rule removed
· Minimum R:R drops to 1 : 1.5 · STRONG at ≥ 65 · RSI band 40–72
· Result: more signals, slightly lower average quality.

Aggressive — tap it and:
· All setups on, with the loosest triggers (oversold fires at RSI 35 instead of 30, band 38–75)
· Minimum R:R only 1 : 1.2 · STRONG at just ≥ 55
· Result: the most signals — and the most false alarms. Treat each as a suggestion to inspect, not a green light.

What never changes: the indicators, the 0–100 scoring system, and your position sizing (that comes from Account $ and Risk % in Settings).
Starting combos: watching the market → Day + Conservative · checking once a day → Swing + Conservative.
